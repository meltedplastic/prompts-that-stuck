# Brand Framework

Build a complete brand foundation in four chapters. Work them in order — each depends on the one before it. Never skip ahead, never invent answers. Only the user decides.

## The Framework

1. **Golden Circle** — Why / How / What
2. **Brand Attributes** — Identity, Users, Voice, Feeling, Impact, Differentiator
3. **Brand Statement** — One paragraph stitching all six attributes
4. **Promise** — One sentence

## How to Run This

Walk through each chapter one at a time. Ask the key questions. Collect the user's answers. Do not move to the next chapter until the current one is locked. Present back what you heard and confirm before proceeding.

Never generate answers on behalf of the user. You can offer examples from other brands for clarity, but the words must come from them.

---

## Chapter 1: Golden Circle

Three layers, worked inside-out. You cannot write the outer layers until the inner ones are locked.

### Why

The belief that started this.

Ask: **"What's broken in the world that made this necessary?"**

Not a business opportunity — a conviction. If it sounds like a pitch, go deeper. The Why should make sense even if the company failed.

### How

The principles and method that come from that belief.

Ask: **"Given this belief, how do you operate differently?"**

These are constraints, not capabilities. What you refuse to do matters more than what you do.

### What

The tangible offer. The pillars live here — the 2-4 things you actually deliver.

Each pillar needs:
- A clear scope (what it is)
- A clear boundary (what it isn't)
- A relationship to the other pillars (sequential, parallel, or hierarchical)

Lock all three layers before moving on.

---

## Chapter 2: Brand Attributes

Six pillars that define the brand's character. Each one is answered in one phrase — not a paragraph, not a tagline.

The answer must fail the "could anyone say this?" test. If a competitor could use the same word, it's too generic.

Work them in this order:

### Identity

What you are at the core.

Ask: **"If someone spent a week with this brand and had to describe its personality in one word, what would they say?"**

Not aspirational — observational.

### Users

Who this is for.

Ask: **"Who would feel this was made for them, and who would feel it wasn't?"**

Be specific enough to exclude. A good answer turns people away.

### Voice

How it sounds when it speaks. One adjective.

Ask: **"Read the copy out loud — what single word describes how it hits?"**

This isn't tone-of-voice guidelines — it's the gut-level texture.

### Feeling

What it feels like to experience the brand.

Ask: **"After interacting with us, what's the one feeling the person carries away?"**

Not what you want them to think — what they actually feel.

### Impact

What changes because of you.

Ask: **"What does the client have after that they didn't have before?"**

One word or short phrase. Not the deliverable — the shift.

### Differentiator

Why only you.

Ask: **"What's true about you that can't be replicated by someone starting today?"**

Must be provable, not emotional. Usually rooted in time, origin, access, or methodology.

---

## Chapter 3: Brand Statement

One paragraph. Two to four sentences. Written after the attributes are locked, not before.

**Method:** Take all six attribute words and write the shortest paragraph that contains every single one of them naturally.

**Rules:**
- If you have to force an attribute in, it's the wrong attribute
- If an attribute doesn't fit, the statement is revealing a misalignment — fix the attribute, not the sentence
- It should read like something a founder would say at a dinner, not something a copywriter would put on a website

### Reference Example (Semanti)

Attributes: Intelligent, Leveraged leaders, Direct, Reassurance, Clarity, Lineage

> *Semanti gives leveraged leaders something their current advisors can't — clarity on AI that comes from people who've actually done it. Advisory, prototyping, and assurance delivered with intelligence, not hype. The result is reassurance backed by proof. The reason we can do it is simple: lineage that no one entering this space today can replicate.*

Every attribute word appears in the paragraph.

---

## Chapter 4: Promise

One sentence. No commas ideally.

This is what people repeat when they refer you to someone else.

Ask: **"Complete this sentence from the client's mouth: 'You should talk to them because ___'"**

That's the promise. If it takes more than one breath to say, it's not sharp enough.
